## Post Content

The hardest part of a job search isn't writing code. It's writing the first message.

I've been on both sides of the hiring table. And here's what I've learned:

Most talented developers stay invisible. Not because they lack skill, but because they wait to be discovered instead of showing up where decisions are made.

My job search journey taught me three things the hard way:

1. Your portfolio speaks louder than your resume. I stopped listing technologies and started showing systems I built — APIs that handle thousands of requests, AI pipelines that run autonomously, dashboards that save teams hours every week.

2. Outreach beats applications. Cold applying into a void has the lowest ROI of any strategy. One thoughtful message to the right person outperforms 200 generic applications.

3. Build in public. Every project I shared, every problem I solved openly, became a magnet for the right conversations. People hire people they've already seen think.

I'm a Full Stack Developer and AI Engineer. I build systems that work while you sleep. But getting to the point where the right people knew that? That took more iteration than any codebase I've shipped.

If you're hiring or searching right now, I'd genuinely love to hear:

What's the one thing that actually moved the needle in your job search or hiring process?
